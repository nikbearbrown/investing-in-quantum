## Chapter summary (from TIKTOC.md)
**Decision question:** If you can't responsibly bet on quantum yet, why own any of it? **Short answer:** Own a *tiny* position (1–10 shares) as an **epistemic** instrument, not a financial bet. **Framework:** A bookmark position changes the quality of your attention — once you own even a sliver, you read the earnings call, track the fidelity numbers, learn the domain. The position must be small enough to pass the **"fine if it goes to zero" test** so it never pressures you to rationalize (the confirmation-bias constraint): a 50% drawdown should produce curiosity, not motivated reasoning. Companies worth *bookmarking* (not betting): IonQ, Quantinuum (post-IPO), QuEra (when accessible). Existing-portfolio exposure for most readers already comes via IBM and Alphabet/Google. **Stay away from** NISQ-era commercial-revenue narratives, LLM-acceleration claims, and weak-differentiation plays (Rigetti, D-Wave).

## A. Conceptual foundations

### The bookmark as attention technology, not capital allocation
The thesis inverts normal position-sizing. The *purpose* of the trade is not return; it is to install a standing reason to pay attention. Humans track what they own. A single share of IonQ turns abstract "quantum news" into "news about my thing," which recruits the domain-learning the book wants the reader to do over the 2026–2032 window. The dollar amount is deliberately trivial — the position is a *bookmark*, a place-holder for attention.
**Common misconception:** "If it's worth owning at all, own a meaningful amount." No — a meaningful amount reintroduces the financial-pressure problem the bookmark is designed to avoid.
**Worked example:** 5 shares of IONQ at ~$40–60 is lunch money; it still earns you a quarterly reason to read the 10-Q and learn what "two-qubit gate fidelity" means.
**Source(s):** position-sizing/skin-in-the-game literature; chapter's own framing.

### The "fine if it goes to zero" test (the confirmation-bias constraint)
Skin in the game sharpens attention — but *too much* skin corrupts it. Once a position is large enough that a loss hurts, the owner starts seeking confirming evidence and dismissing disconfirming evidence (motivated reasoning). The bookmark sits below that threshold by construction. The test is operational: if a 50% overnight decline would tempt you to write a Reddit post defending the company, the position is too big.
**Common misconception:** that "conviction" requires size. Conviction here is epistemic, not financial.
**Source(s):** Taleb on skin in the game; behavioral-finance literature on disposition effect / motivated reasoning.

### Bookmark vs. bet vs. ambient exposure
Three distinct relationships: (1) **bet** — sized to matter, demands a thesis and an exit; (2) **bookmark** — sized to be ignorable financially but unignorable attentionally; (3) **ambient exposure** — quantum you already own without choosing it, via index funds holding IBM and Alphabet. The chapter's claim is that most readers should sit at (2) and (3), never (1), until the four-part signal fires.
**Worked example:** An S&P 500 index holder already owns IBM (superconducting Heron/Nighthawk) and Alphabet (Google Willow). That is ambient quantum exposure requiring zero action.
**Source(s):** IBM Quantum hardware page; Google Willow coverage.

## B. Domain examples and cases

### Case 1 — IonQ (NYSE: IONQ), the financial-milestone outlier (VERIFIED)
Trapped ion. **FY2025 revenue ~$130M, +202% YoY — the first quantum company to exceed $100M in GAAP revenue** (IonQ frames itself as first to seven, eight, and nine figures). Acquisitions: **Oxford Ionics for ~$1.075B** (stock + ~$10M cash, closed Sept 17 2025) and **SkyWater Technology for ~$1.8B** (announced Jan 26 2026; combined ~3,000 employees). This is the clearest "bookmark" candidate because its disclosures are richest. *Caveat for the chapter:* >$100M revenue is a milestone of commercial *traction*, not of fault-tolerant *advantage* — keep the distinction sharp.

### Case 2 — Quantinuum (trapped ion, Honeywell-backed) — IPO is real (VERIFIED)
Quantinuum **filed to go public in 2026**: incorporated as a Delaware corp Jan 20 2026, with S-1, S-1/A, and a 424B4 prospectus on file (the 424B4 indicates a priced/near-priced offering). Highest two-qubit gate fidelity in the industry (industry-standard performance metric); manufacturing/quality discipline inherited from Honeywell. Private until the IPO closes; bookmark *when shares are accessible*.

### Case 3 — QuEra (neutral atom, private) — the logical-qubit record holder (VERIFIED)
**96 logical qubits from 448 physical atoms, [[16,6,4]] codes, below-threshold, published in Nature, January 2026** — first demonstration of >48 verified logical qubits, doubling the prior record in ~13 months, with error-corrected gates across all 96 simultaneously. Backed by **Google Quantum AI–led $230M round** alongside SoftBank Vision Fund 2 and NVIDIA Ventures. Private — cannot be bookmarked as a stock; watch as the technical pace-setter.

### Failure case — the "stay away" list (VERIFIED valuations, mid-2026)
- **Rigetti (RGTI):** Q1 2026 revenue ~$4.4M against an ~$8B market cap; ~$569M cash runway. Valuation untethered from revenue; no clear architectural differentiation. Stay away.
- **D-Wave (QBTS):** market cap ~$5.27B (June 4 2026); FY2025 revenue ~$24.6M (+179%), 83% gross margin; $550M acquisition of Quantum Circuits Inc. makes it the only dual-platform (annealing + gate) player. **Annealing utility is contested** (see Ch 6 / Flatiron classical-simulation work). Stay away as a *quantum-advantage* bet.
- Both: classic NISQ-era commercial-revenue narratives priced for a future that the science has not yet delivered.

## E. Teaching considerations
**Where readers get stuck:** the counterintuitive claim that *smaller is better*. Readers want "how much should I buy"; the answer ("the least that still makes you read the earnings call") feels wrong.
**Analogies that work:** a bookmark in a book you intend to finish; a gym membership you bought to make yourself go; owning a single share so the company sends you the annual report.
**Exercises:** (1) Apply the "fine if it goes to zero" test to your own net worth — compute the share count that passes. (2) List your existing index funds and find your *ambient* IBM/Alphabet quantum exposure (you may already own more than you'd buy deliberately). (3) Take one "stay away" company and write the bull case, then find the disconfirming fidelity/revenue number that kills it.
